Pick the crank before measuring — Crank Journal Size in the job cardThe IH shop manual gives main 3.3742–3.3755 in and rod 2.9977–2.9990 in. The DT466E sheet and Heavy Duty Pros give main 3.535 in and rod 3.150 in for later DT466B/C engines. They are about 0.16 in apart, so one micrometer reading tells you which crank you have. Until you choose, journal diameter rows show SET CRANK — not PASS or FAIL.
Undersize Journals (both main & rod)Set Crank Grind in the job card and the journal limits shift down by the undersize (.010 / .020 / .030 in). SRC lists .020" NARROW main and rod bearing sets (N104219 / N104220) — confirm the crank grind before ordering.
Source NoteOut-of-round (0.0013 in), end play and thrust location are from the IH 400-series shop manual (Jensales IH-S-ENG3/400, p.1-34). The IH manual gives no taper limit, so taper is recorded only.
Crankshaft Inspection Procedure
  1. Mark main caps for position and direction before removal. Lift crank out with a sling at the centre journals — never by a single counterweight.
  2. Clean the crank with solvent. Brush every oil drilling, then blow dry (max 30 psi shop air).
  3. Crack-check the crank (magnaflux) at the fillets and oil holes. Any crack = reject.
  4. Read the casting number and measure one main and one rod journal width. Confirm narrow (1.230" / 1.200") or wide (1.294" / 1.260").
  5. Measure one main and one rod journal. About 3.37 / 3.00 in = IH crank; about 3.53 / 3.15 in = DT466B/C crank. Set Crank Journal Size in the job card.
  6. Check the front of the crank or counterweight for an undersize stamp. Set Crank Grind in the job card to match.
  7. Measure each main journal in two planes (A and B, 90° apart) at both ends. Enter the largest diameter in the table.
  8. Enter the two plane readings in the A/B boxes for out-of-round. Enter front-end and rear-end readings for taper. The sheet works out the difference.
  9. Repeat for all six rod journals.
  10. Inspect the front seal journal for a groove. SRC part N104001 is a front seal with wear sleeve.
  11. Inspect the crank gear, keyway and pulley bolt threads. Crank pulley torque is 105 ft-lb.
  12. If not installing right away, oil the journals and cover the crank with plastic.

Torque confirmed — 115 ft-lbThe IH shop manual torque chart (p.1-2) and SRC manual p.33 agree on 115 ft-lb (156 N·m). The 130 ft-lb and 100 → 130 + 90° figures on DT466E sheets are for a later engine — do not use them here. The 60 ft-lb first step is TSI shop practice, not an OEM step.
Critical CheckThe crank must turn freely by hand after each cap is torqued. If it binds, stop and find out why before going on.
Main Bearing Installation Procedure
  1. Clean saddles, caps and bolt holes. No burrs or raised metal. Never use a wire brush on bearing shells.
  2. Install new shells (grind size must match Section 1). Upper shells with oil holes go in the block. Check lock tangs seat in their notches.
  3. Torque caps to final with no crank installed. Measure and record each bore ID (dial bore gauge).
  4. Work out oil clearance: bore ID − journal OD. Record in the table. Spec 0.0018–0.0051".
  5. Remove caps. Oil the shells with clean engine oil and lower the crank in.
  6. Install caps in their marked positions and direction. Oil the bolt threads and under the heads.
  7. Torque centre cap first, working outward, to 115 ft-lb. Turn the crank by hand after each cap.
  8. Pry the crank fully rearward, zero a dial indicator on the crank nose, then pry forward. Record end play. Spec 0.006–0.012" (thrust bearing at the REAR main; reject over 0.020").

Connecting Rod Installation Procedure
  1. Mark each rod and cap with cylinder number before removal. Keep caps with their own rods.
  2. Check each rod for twist and bend, and check small-end bushing fit with the new pin.
  3. Install new shells (grind size matches Section 1). Lock tangs in notches.
  4. Torque the cap to 130 ft-lb with no crank. Measure and record the bore ID.
  5. Work out oil clearance: bore ID − rod journal OD. Spec 0.0018–0.0051".
  6. Assemble piston to rod (Section 4). Fit both pin retainers fully into their grooves.
  7. Oil the shells and journal. Install the rod and cap, matching numbers and direction.
  8. Torque the rod bolts to 130 ft-lb. Turn the crank by hand.
  9. Measure side clearance with a feeler gauge between the rod pair and crank cheek. Spec 0.007–0.015".

Chrome ringsDo NOT file chrome rings to open the gap — the chrome chips. Each 0.001 in of bore wear adds about 0.0032 in of gap. The intermediate gap depends on which ring is in the kit — check the kit sheet and note which type.
Piston protrusionIH diesel piston protrusion (0.0029–0.0201 in, DT-466) does not apply to the SRC natural gas piston. Record it only.
Sleeve Protrusion — measure before the head goes on
  1. Clean the block counterbore and sleeve flange. Any dirt changes the reading.
  2. Install the sleeve without lower O-rings for the check. Clamp it down with a sleeve hold-down plate.
  3. Zero a dial indicator on the block deck. Read sleeve height at 4 points, 90° apart.
  4. Record the lowest of the 4 readings for each cylinder. Spec 0.002–0.005".
  5. Too low: add shims under the flange (.002 / .004 / .010 / .020 / .032"). Too high: check the counterbore for dirt or a raised edge.
  6. A counterbore that is cracked, eroded or out of square must be machined and shimmed.
Adjacent Cylinder DifferenceNavistar DT466E data allows 0.001" variation. No figure was found for this early block, so the difference is recorded for reference only.
Piston & Ring Installation Procedure
  1. Inspect the lower O-ring bores for cavitation pitting. Pitting under the O-ring path = machine or sleeve the block.
  2. Fit new lower O-rings N104222. Lube with clean engine oil or the lube supplied in the kit — never petroleum grease on silicone.
  3. Press the sleeve in evenly by hand pressure or a sleeve driver. Recheck protrusion after the final install.
  4. Check every piston part number is N104108 (NG, 8.5:1).
  5. Check ring end gap in the sleeve, square to the bore, with a feeler gauge. Record. Compare to the ring kit sheet.
  6. Fit rings with the identification mark facing up. Stagger the gaps evenly around the piston — never line them up.
  7. Oil the sleeve, piston and rings. Use a ring compressor and tap the piston in with a wooden handle.
  8. Measure piston height at TDC with a dial indicator (record only).

Cylinder Head Inspection Procedure
  1. SRC calls for a head exchange at 9,000 hours. If you are fitting an exchange or rebuilt head, get the shop paperwork (pressure test, valve job, seat material).
  2. Clean carbon from the deck and ports. Look for cracks between valve seats and around plug bores.
  3. Measure deck-to-deck thickness (valve cover face to gasket face) at both ends and the centre. Record the thinnest. Minimum 4.188 in.
  4. Flatness: straight edge across the width — resurface if a 0.004 in feeler fits; then 5 lengthwise positions — resurface if a 0.006 in feeler fits. Never remove more than 0.010 in.
  5. Valve recession on all 12 valves: 0.000–0.014 in. Over the limit = new seat inserts. Guide ID 0.3740–0.3750 in. Springs: 156–164 lb at 1.56 in.
  6. Check deck flatness with a straight edge and feeler gauge, lengthwise and corner to corner.
  7. Measure valve head recession on all 12 valves with a depth gauge. Record.
  8. Clean the spark plug threads with a thread chaser. Check plug wells for oil or coolant.
  9. Check the head part number is N104011 (466TA natural gas head).

Notes & Cautions
IH steps the old checklist was missingBlow out the bolt holes first (trapped fluid can crack the block). Back off the lash adjusting screws before tightening (avoids bent pushrods). When tightening the outer bolts that hold the rocker support brackets, put a 0.005 in feeler between the bracket and the outer rocker arm. After Stage 1, check the two end rockers move freely. Never back a bolt off — pull up to the next torque. Re-torque the head after the first hour of running and recheck lash.
Final value settled — 165 ft-lbThe IH shop manual gives 165 ft-lb final. SRC's 170 ft-lb matches the IH chart line for the exhaust elbow and regulator cap nut, so it is treated as a copy error.
Old SRC note (kept for reference)SRC manual p.33 gives cylinder head bolts as 170 ft-lb with no sequence. The 110 / 155 / 165 ft-lb three-stage method is the Navistar procedure for this head bolt layout and is also listed for the early DT466 by Heavy Duty Pros (shown there as 110 / 115 / 165 — the middle value is likely a misprint of 155). Confirm the final stage value with ComGen before torquing if SRC-supplied bolts are used.
Do not re-use stretched boltsCheck every bolt for necking or thread damage. Replace any bolt that is suspect.
Spark plugsInstall plugs after the head is torqued: gap 0.015", anti-seize, 20 ft-lb.

Lash Interval (SRC)Set lash at overhaul, again at 750 hours (one time), then every 1,500 hours. Replace the valve cover gasket each time.
Warm or cold?The IH lash chart is for an engine at operating temperature (warm). SRC gives 0.025 in but does not say warm or cold. Until SRC confirms, set it warm per IH and note the temperature. Never use a feeler gauge with the engine running.
Source NoteCam, gear backlash and rocker figures are from the IH 400-series shop manual.
Valve Lash — Two-Position Method (SRC p.16–17)
  1. Engine at operating temperature (IH: engine warm), stopped. Remove the valve cover.
  2. Turn the crank until #1 is at TDC compression and the pointer lines up with the TDC mark. Both #1 pushrods turn freely by hand.
  3. Set the six valves in the first table to 0.025". Slight drag on the feeler gauge is correct.
  4. Hold the screw, tighten the locknut to 20 ft-lb, and recheck.
  5. Turn the crank one full turn (360°) to #6 TDC compression. Set the six valves in the second table.
  6. Fit a new gasket and grommets. Make sure the cover sits flat and is not resting on nearby bolts or washers. Torque to spec.

Camshaft & Gear Train
  1. Inspect cam lobes and journals for pitting, scuffing or flat spots. Inspect lifter faces.
  2. Check cam bushings for scoring and correct oil hole alignment.
  3. Fit the thrust plate and gear. Measure cam end play with a dial indicator. Record.

Lubrication & Cooling Procedure
  1. Strip, clean and inspect the oil pump. Check gear teeth, housing pockets and cover face for scoring.
  2. Gerotor pump: measure outer rotor end clearance (0.0015–0.0040 in), inner rotor end clearance (0.0018–0.0044 in) and outer rotor radial clearance to housing (0.0055–0.0085 in).
  3. Fit a new oil pressure regulator valve (N104218) and a new pickup tube gasket (N104223). Clean the pickup screen.
  4. Clean and pressure test the oil cooler. Fit a new cooler gasket (N104643).
  5. Fit the oil pan with a new gasket (N671827C2). Check the remote drain hose and valve.
  6. Fit a new water pump (N104008) and 180°F thermostat (N103997) with a new housing gasket (N103542).
  7. Replace radiator hoses (N103670 / N103671), bypass hose (N672242C2) and clamps.
  8. Fill with the correct oil and coolant mix. Prime the oil system before first crank.
  9. If a Kenco oil level control is fitted, check the 26" hoses and set the float level.

First Start Procedure (SRC p.10–11)
  1. Never use starting fluid on this engine.
  2. Check oil level, coolant level, belts and that nothing is touching moving parts.
  3. 466TA: pre-lube the turbo — fill the oil inlet with clean oil before connecting the feed line.
  4. Key on, engine off: the Check Engine light must come on.
  5. Crank no more than 30 seconds. Wait 2 minutes between attempts. About 4 seconds of cranking is normal.
  6. Oil pressure must show within 15 seconds. If not, shut down and find out why. Hot: at least 15 psi at idle, 45–65 psi at rated rpm (IH).
  7. Idle at least 1 minute before load. Check for fuel, coolant and oil leaks.
  8. Set and lock ignition timing for the fuel being used. Record it.
  9. Shutdown after load: idle 5 minutes with no load. This is critical on the turbo engine.

⚠ CAUTION Monitor oil pressure, coolant temperature, and oil temperature continuously during the break-in run. Shut down immediately if any parameter exceeds limits. Do not leave the unit unattended during the first hour.
SRC Break-In Rules — first 100 hoursRun at one-half to three-quarters load. Do not run at full rated load for more than 5 minutes at a time. Do not idle or run unloaded for long periods (glazing). Low-load limits: 0–30% for ½ h · 31–40% for 2 h · 41–50% for 8 h · 51–60% for 12 h · 61–100% continuous. After any low-load period, run at least 2 hours above 70%.
Calculator noteThe calculator sets the load bank to 75% of engine HP as electrical kW. Generator losses mean the crankshaft load will be a little above 75%. Do not set the bank above the calculated value on a fresh engine.
Load Bank Break-In Procedure
  1. Verify engine oil level, coolant level, and fuel supply before starting.
  2. Start engine (no starting fluid). Verify oil pressure within 15 seconds (SRC). Warm up at idle at least 1 minute, then at light load until coolant reaches 180°F.
  3. Connect load bank to generator output terminals. Verify correct voltage and phase rotation before applying load.
  4. Use the Break-In Calculator below to determine target amperage. Set load bank to 75% of engine rated HP.
  5. Apply load gradually — bring to 25%, then 50%, then 75% over 5–10 minutes.
  6. Begin recording data at T+0:30 (30 minutes after reaching full load). Record all parameters every 30 minutes for 4 hours (8 readings total).
  7. Monitor for oil leaks, coolant leaks, unusual noises, or abnormal vibration throughout the run.
  8. At completion, reduce load gradually to zero, then idle with no load for 5 minutes before shutdown (critical on 466TA turbo).
  9. After shutdown, check oil level, coolant level, and inspect for any leaks or loose connections.
  10. Record final ignition timing and verify it has not drifted during the run.
Acceptance Criteria — All must be met to pass
  1. Oil Pressure: 45–65 PSI at rated RPM, oil hot (IH manual). No downward trend.
  2. Coolant Temperature: Stable in the 180–200°F normal range. Shut down above 215°F (SRC).
  3. Oil Temperature: Stable below 250°F. Should stabilize within first hour.
  4. Voltage: All three phases within ±2% of rated voltage throughout run.
  5. Frequency: 60 Hz ±0.5 Hz throughout run.
  6. kW Output: Stable at or above 75% of rated kW throughout run.
  7. Battery Voltage: 13.2–14.8V (charging) throughout run.
  8. Fuel Pressure: Stable — no significant drop during run indicating fuel supply issue.
  9. No leaks of oil, coolant, or fuel at any point during or after the run.
  10. Ignition Timing: Within ±2° of the value set for this fuel (Section 9) at end of run.
  11. Frequency stability: SRC governor regulation ±0.5% no-load to full-load.
